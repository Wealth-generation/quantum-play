## Phase 4D Decision Lock

- Audit decision: the Phase 4B/4C target/corridor-guided visible runtime is product-invalid and rejected as the primary Plinko animation core. It cannot meet the approved free-fall, contact-driven visual goal through further tuning.
- Root cause: primary motion is built from target-aligned corridor bands, fixed-step horizontal guidance, and velocity clamping. Its target-only builder emits no physical peg contacts, so Pixi cannot render real per-peg response.
- Replacement direction: implement a renderer-local bounce/contact-driven Matter candidate simulation as the next primary visible playback core. This is a full visible-core replacement, not another tuning pass, corridor widening pass, launch-spread pass, safer guided-trajectory iteration, small patch, or cautious preservation of the current model.
- Candidate-only first: generate bounded deterministic launch candidates from the visual seed, simulate gravity and actual peg collisions, record physical contacts, and select only a naturally target-reaching candidate. Do not enable lower-board capture, hidden steering, snap, teleport, magneting, corridor guidance, or row-by-row backend checkpoints in the first replacement implementation.
- Current model status: `plinko-corridor-plan.ts` and `matter-plinko-target-trajectory.ts` must leave primary visible runtime. They may be referenced only for cleanup, fallback isolation, or historical context; upper/middle corridor guidance must not survive as the new core.
- Legacy fallback policy: legacy Matter/path and motion-plan playback may remain only as temporary development safety during the rebuild. A legacy fallback during normal manual QA is a bounce-core failure, not a passing product result.
- Invalid target policy: if bounded candidate-only physics cannot produce valid target-reaching trajectories often enough, report the measured validity failure, explain candidate causes, present options, recommend one, and stop for explicit human approval. Do not silently add assist, steering, corridor forces, capture, snap, or magneting.
- Lower-board assist policy: no lower-board assist/capture zone is approved for the first replacement step. It may be considered only after measured candidate-only QA failure and explicit human approval; any later assist must be late, subtle, lower-board-only, and non-magnetic.
- Contact core requirements: produce renderer-local per-ball contact data with peg identity, row/index when available, impact strength, directional cue, timing, and optional bounded contact-driven micro-stall/perch marker. Pixi peg visual response is a core replacement requirement, not optional polish.
- Micro-stall policy: a short visual perch marker is allowed only after a measured low-energy, non-glancing peg contact. It must stay bounded, rare, per-ball, and unrelated to settlement authority; it must not be manufactured by rewriting the sampled trajectory timeline.
- Authority and scope: accepted backend result remains authoritative and may derive only the final target bucket. The replacement must not use backend row-by-row `results` as a visual path or change BFF/API, result contracts, payout, balance, history, fairness, AutoBet pacing, layout, shell, dependencies, board geometry, or current multi-ball interaction scope.
- Future-ready constraint: do not add ball-ball collision, but keep playback state per round/per ball rather than assuming one global ball.
